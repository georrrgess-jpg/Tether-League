# 01 — Game vision

## Pitch

Tether League is a bright, approachable robot sport where every anchor can turn a loose ball into a brilliant play. Run, swing, release, pass, and score. Build a club from a modest seaside team into a championship contender.

The first-time player understands the objective immediately. The intermediate player discovers better approaches and passes. The expert learns to manipulate space, bait challenges, preserve momentum, and recover before the opponent can counterattack.

## Audience and sessions

Design for players who like arcade sports, satisfying movement, short competitive matches, collection, and visible progress. Primary use is a browser on a PC or Chromebook, with phone support designed into input and layout. A normal session can be one three-minute match plus short transitions; a longer session can finish part of a season or practice a new skill.

The desired return loop is: play a good match, understand what improved, receive a concrete earned reward, see the next club objective, and choose another useful activity. Fun and mastery must carry the experience even after unlocks are exhausted.

## Five design pillars

1. **Movement creates opportunities.** Tethering changes how a player reaches the ball, rather than functioning as a decorative dash.
2. **A readable team sport.** The ball, goals, four robots, teams, and selected anchor remain clear at normal play speed.
3. **Useful decisions at all skill levels.** Beginners can run and kick; stronger players use swing timing, open-space passes, and fast recovery.
4. **Progress that belongs to the player.** The club, garage, records, trophies, and appearance preserve a history of earned accomplishments.
5. **A finished, efficient experience.** Responsive control and consistent menus matter more than excessive spectacle.

## The first ten minutes

- Club creation presents a name, two colors, and a simple badge choice, with useful defaults.
- A skippable interactive exercise teaches moving, acquiring an anchor, holding, releasing, and kicking.
- A forgiving practice goal gives an immediate success without pretending it was a competitive win.
- The first bot match introduces the teammate and shows a single contextual hint at a time.
- Results explain the score, a useful contribution, earned credits/XP, and the next match.
- One early cosmetic choice lets the player personalize the club quickly.

## Main loop

Choose a match or trial → play → receive real results → earn club progress and credits → customize or practice → continue the season.

The career is the long goal. Skill trials provide a small goal between matches. Chassis choices provide experimentation. Appearance rewards provide ownership without competitive stat inflation.

## Boundaries

The first release is a single-player browser game with reliable bots and local saves. Online play, full aerial physics, complex management, a marketplace, and a large narrative campaign are later possibilities. A small number of carefully differentiated arenas is more valuable than many decorative variants.

## Creative latitude

The developer can revise this structure freely when there is a stronger practical design. Use playtests to decide whether a mechanic belongs. The core requirement is a satisfying sport built around tether movement and club progression, not adherence to every suggested detail.
